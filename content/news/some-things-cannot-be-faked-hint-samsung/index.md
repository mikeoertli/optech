Samsung isn't exactly know for their design prowess or their honesty. They've been [caught paying people to leave fake negative reviews](http://www.theverge.com/2013/10/24/5023658/samsung-fined-340000-for-posting-negative-htc-reviews) on competitor's products, they've been [caught rigging their devices to perform better](http://gizmodo.com/samsung-rigged-the-s4-to-unnaturally-perform-better-in-971577921) on benchmarks than they do in real life, and they've been [caught stealing design cues](http://samsungcopiesapple.tumblr.com) from Apple (though rarely nicely implemented).

*Side note: everyone in tech steals things from everyone else. There are a limited number of layouts, gestures, and features. There will be overlap. Don't read into that one too much, I couldn't resist posting a link to that Tumblr page.*

What does this all add up to? The first thing that comes to mind is desperation. It is a shame, they're doing some decent things. Their phones are selling in record numbers and they're the only Android device maker that's [actually making any money](http://appleinsider.com/articles/13/11/14/apple-samsung-take-massive-109-of-mobile-industry-profits-while-competitors-lose-money). Then they go and do things like [fake leather with fake stitching](http://www.engadget.com/2014/03/03/samsung-chromebook-2/?ncid=rss_truncated) on their products and paying a boat load of money for celebrity endorsements who promptly [return to their iPhones](http://marketingland.com/ellen-oscars-samsung-iphone-75651) when the camera turns off.

Loyalty cannot be faked; it can be purchased in the short term, but it won't last. The simplest description I can come up with is that this is the difference between being passionate about products and being passionate about your reputation at all costs. They'll keep doing these things until the market votes for a company that doesn't, for example by buying a Moto X instead of an S5.
